Yes. Count your home at a rough sale value and subtract what's left on the mortgage. That's the standard definition, and it's what national surveys use. But also look at your net worth without the home, because you can't spend a house you live in. The two numbers answer different questions.

Ask ten people whether their home counts as part of their net worth and you'll get two camps. One says of course, it's the most valuable thing we own. The other says it doesn't count, because we have to live somewhere. Both are partly right.

## Why the home is such a big part of net worth

For most families who own one, the home is the largest thing they have. In the US Federal Reserve's [2025 Survey of Consumer Finances](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/scf26.pdf), about 66% of families owned their home. Among them, the median home was worth $375,000, and the median home equity (the value minus the mortgage and other home loans) was $230,000.

[Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/2023/12/04/the-assets-households-own-and-the-debts-they-carry/), using 2021 data, found that home equity made up a median of 45% of homeowners' net worth. So for a typical owner, leaving the home out roughly halves the number.

## Two numbers, two questions

| Number | What it answers | Good for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **With the home** | How much do we own, all in? | The full picture; comparing with official figures |
| **Without the home** | How much could we use without moving? | Planning; how much freedom your money gives you |

The second number is the one that matters for anything you want to fund from your savings, from a work break to retiring. It's also the one used for a freedom number, because you'd still need somewhere to live.

## A worked example

**EXAMPLE:** a made-up couple who own their flat together. The numbers are invented to show the math.

| Item | Own | Owe |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Flat (rough sale value) | 320,000 | |
| Mortgage left | | 210,000 |
| Savings and investments | 48,000 | |
| Pensions with a balance | 37,000 | |
| Car loan | | 6,000 |

- **With the home:** 405,000 − 216,000 = **189,000**
- **Without the home:** 85,000 − 6,000 = **79,000**

The flat accounts for 110,000 of their 189,000, more than half. If house prices in their area rise 10%, their net worth jumps by 32,000 without them doing anything. If prices fall, it drops just as fast. Without the home, their number moves only when they save, pay off debt, or their investments change.

## How to value your home

- **Use a rough sale value, not what you paid.** Look at recent sale prices of similar homes nearby, or an online estimate.
- **Be a little cautious.** Take a figure toward the lower end of the range. You're tracking, not selling.
- **Update it once or twice a year.** Home prices move slowly and estimates jump around. Changing the value every month adds noise to a number you want to read clearly.
- **Leave out selling costs,** unless you're actually planning to sell. If you are, subtract them, because agent fees and taxes are real.

## What if you rent?

Then there's nothing to decide: your net worth already is the "without the home" number. Renters often feel they're behind because headline figures include homes. In the same Fed survey, the median net worth of renters was $10,600, against $458,900 for owners. Compare yourself only with your own last month.

## For couples: whose home is it?

- **A home you bought together,** with a mortgage you both signed, belongs in Ours, with the mortgage next to it.
- **A home one of you owned before,** in one name, usually sits in that person's Mine, even if you both live there. Some couples move it to Ours by agreement.
- **One pays the mortgage, both live there.** Count the home and the loan where the ownership is, and talk about the payments separately. Who legally owns what depends on your country and your situation; that's a question for a local adviser, not a tracker.

## So which number should you track?

Both, side by side. The full number with the home shows what you've built. The number without it shows what your money can do for you. If you can only track one, track the full number and keep the home's value deliberately steady, so the monthly changes you see come from what you actually do.

::: faq
### Do official net worth figures include the home?
Yes. Surveys like the Fed's Survey of Consumer Finances count the home's value minus home loans as part of net worth.

### Should I count a second home or rental property?
Yes, at a rough sale value minus its loan. Unlike your own home, you could sell it without moving, so it also belongs in the "without the home" number.

### What about the car?
Many people count it at a rough sale value, but it loses value every year. Counting it or not is fine; just be consistent.
:::
