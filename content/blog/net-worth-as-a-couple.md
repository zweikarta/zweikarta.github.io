Ask a couple how much they own together and most will look at each other and guess. It isn't carelessness. The numbers live in different apps and two different heads, and nobody has ever added them up.

## What counts as net worth?

Net worth is one number: the value of everything you own, minus everything you owe.

**What you own − what you owe = net worth**

**What you own:** bank and savings accounts, investments, pensions with a balance, and your home or car at a rough sale value.

**What you owe:** your mortgage, car and student loans, and card balances you don't clear each month.

It isn't your income. A couple can earn a lot and have a small net worth, or the other way round.

## Three ways to count it as a couple

Couples set up their money very differently. In a [December 2025 Bankrate survey](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/02/12/most-couples-keep-at-least-some-of-their-money-separate-survey-reveals/178000/) of US adults with a partner, 38% had fully combined their finances, 26% kept them fully separate and 36% used a mix. How you count can follow the same setup.

![Three ways to count: one household number, Ours plus each partner's Mine, or two separate numbers.](three-ways.png "Three ways to count net worth as a couple. Most couples fit the middle one.")

| Way | What you count | Watch out for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **One household number** | Everything either of you owns, minus everything either of you owes | One of you may feel their private savings disappear into "ours" |
| **Ours + Mine** | A shared number for what you own together, plus a private number each | You need to agree what counts as "ours" |
| **Two separate numbers** | Each of you counts only what's in your own name | Shared things, like a home or a loan, get counted twice or not at all |

We like **Ours + Mine** for most couples, because it works whatever your setup. You can add the three numbers together whenever you both want to, but you never have to.

## How to calculate it, step by step

1. **Write today's date.** Net worth is a snapshot you'll compare later.
2. **List what you own.** Each balance from your banking and pension apps; a rough sale value for a home or car.
3. **List what you owe.** The amount still to pay, not the monthly payment.
4. **Mark each line Ours or Mine.** Joint accounts and loans you both signed go in Ours. Anything in one name goes in that person's Mine.
5. **Add it up.** Ours = shared assets − shared debts. Each Mine = that person's assets − their debts.

## A worked example

**EXAMPLE:** a made-up couple, Partner A and Partner B. The numbers are invented to show the math. The same steps work in any currency.

| Item | Whose | Own | Owe |
| :--- | :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Joint account | Ours | 6,000 | |
| Home (rough sale value) | Ours | 350,000 | |
| Mortgage left | Ours | | 240,000 |
| Car (rough sale value) | Ours | 12,000 | |
| Car loan | Ours | | 8,000 |
| Savings | A's Mine | 9,000 | |
| Retirement account | A's Mine | 41,000 | |
| Student loan | A's Mine | | 15,000 |
| Savings | B's Mine | 4,000 | |
| Investment account | B's Mine | 22,000 | |
| Credit card balance | B's Mine | | 2,500 |

The three numbers:

- **Ours:** 368,000 − 248,000 = **120,000**
- **A's Mine:** 50,000 − 15,000 = **35,000**
- **B's Mine:** 26,000 − 2,500 = **23,500**

If they both choose to share their Mine totals, their household net worth is 120,000 + 35,000 + 23,500 = **178,500**. Notice that most of their Ours is the home. Leave the home out and Ours drops to 10,000. That's why the next question matters.

## The tricky items

### Should you include your house?

Most net worth figures include it: the home's value minus the mortgage left. Because you can't spend a home you live in, many couples also look at the number without it.

### Retirement accounts and pensions

Count any account that shows a balance. A state pension with no cash value is usually left out; what matters is counting it the same way every month.

### Does a partner's debt count?

A debt in one partner's name belongs in their Mine. A loan you both signed belongs in Ours, even if one of you makes the payments.

### Money from family

An inheritance one of you keeps separate goes in that person's Mine. If you agree to share it, move it to Ours.

### What if the number is negative?

It's common early on, with student loans or a new mortgage. It's a starting point, and the change from month to month matters more.

## What's a normal net worth for a couple?

There's no couple-specific figure that's worth much, and comparing is less useful than it feels. For context, the US Federal Reserve's [2022 Survey of Consumer Finances](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/October-2023-changes-in-us-family-finances-from-2019-to-2022.htm) found a median family net worth of $192,900. The mean was $1,063,700, pulled up by a small number of very wealthy families. That's why the median is the figure to look at.

| Age of reference person | Median net worth, 2022 (US) |
| :--- | ---: |
| Under 35 | $39,000 |
| 35–44 | $135,600 |
| 45–54 | $247,200 |
| 55–64 | $364,500 |
| 65–74 | $409,900 |
| 75 and over | $335,600 |

For a couple, the survey uses one partner's age. Figures differ a lot by country and home ownership, so the comparison that tells you most is your own number against last month's.

## How often should you update it?

Once a month is enough. Pick a fixed day and update only the numbers that changed. Then look at one thing together: did Ours grow or dip, and why?

::: faq
### Do married couples combine their net worth?
Many do, and surveys usually measure net worth per household. But you don't have to merge accounts to know where you stand together. Counting a shared Ours and a private Mine each works whether you're married or not. What counts as shared in law depends on your country and situation, which is a question for a local adviser.

### Is it OK to keep some money private from my partner?
Privacy you both agreed on is healthy. Hiding a debt isn't. The difference is that you both know the private part exists.
:::
