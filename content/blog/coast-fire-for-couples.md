Many couples reach a point where one of them wants to change something: work part-time, care for a child or a parent, start something new, or simply rest. The usual answer is "not yet, we need to save more". Coast FIRE is a way to check whether that's actually true.

## What is Coast FIRE?

FIRE stands for financial independence, retire early. Your [freedom number](/blog/freedom-number/) is the amount you'd need for your investments to cover your spending.

Coast FIRE is a step before that. You've reached it when the money you already have, left to grow, would reach your freedom number by a set age. From then on, you only need to earn enough to cover what you spend today. Saving more still helps, but it's no longer required.

## How to work it out

You need three things:

1. **Your freedom number.** Roughly 25 times your yearly spending, less what pensions will cover.
2. **The years until you'd use it.** For example, from now until 60.
3. **A growth rate.** The yearly return you assume, after inflation.

Then: **Coast number = freedom number ÷ (1 + growth)^years**

If what you have invested today is at or above that number, you've reached Coast FIRE, on those assumptions.

## A worked example

**EXAMPLE:** a made-up couple, both 35. The numbers are invented to show the math. Example, not a forecast or advice. Returns aren't guaranteed.

- Their freedom number is **1,200,000** (25 × 48,000 yearly spending).
- They want it available at 60: **25 years** from now.

What they'd need invested today depends a lot on the growth rate they assume:

| Growth after inflation (assumed) | Coast number today |
| ---: | ---: |
| 3% a year | 573,000 |
| 4% a year | 450,000 |
| 5% a year | 354,000 |

If they have 450,000 invested, they've reached Coast FIRE if they assume 4%, but not if they assume 3%. That's the honest lesson of Coast FIRE: the answer is only as good as the growth rate, and nobody knows the future rate. Choose a cautious one, and treat the result as a rough check, not a green light.

Time matters too. At 4% a year, the same couple would need 666,000 invested if they wanted the money in 15 years instead of 25.

## What it means for a couple

For a single person, Coast FIRE means "I could earn less". For a couple, it's a household question with more options:

- **One works less, the other keeps going.** If one income covers your spending, the other partner could step back without touching your investments.
- **Both work less.** Two part-time incomes can cover spending as well as one full-time one.
- **Take turns.** One slows down for a few years, then the other.

Whatever you choose, test it with your own numbers: does the remaining income cover what you spend in a normal month, plus a buffer for the unexpected?

## Questions to talk through together

- Which of us wants to slow down, and why now?
- Can the income we'd keep cover our spending, without dipping into investments?
- What happens to pensions and other benefits when one of us earns less? (Often they shrink. Check how this works where you live.)
- What would make us change course? For example, investments dipping a lot for a long time.
- Is it fair to both of us, and how do we keep it feeling fair?

The last question matters as much as the math. Couples who talk openly about who earns, who saves and who gives up what tend to make these changes work. Our guide on [talking about money as a couple](/blog/how-to-talk-about-money-as-a-couple/) has a simple format for it.

## The risks to be honest about

- **Growth isn't guaranteed.** A few weak years early on can push your target back by years.
- **Lower income can mean smaller pensions.** In many countries, pensions depend on what you pay in while working.
- **Spending changes.** Children, health and housing can raise it. Recheck your freedom number every year.
- **Stopping savings is a choice, not a requirement.** Many people at Coast FIRE keep saving a little, for extra room.

::: faq
### What's the difference between Coast FIRE and FIRE?
FIRE means your investments already cover your spending, so you don't need to work at all. Coast FIRE means your investments are on track to get there, so you only need to earn what you spend today.

### What growth rate should we use?
There's no right answer. Many people use a return after inflation between 3% and 5%, and try more than one. If the plan only works with the highest rate, it's not a safe plan.

### Do we need to reach Coast FIRE together?
It's a household number, so it's usually counted together. What you've each saved can stay in your own name; what matters is whether the total is on track.
:::
